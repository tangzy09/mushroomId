Blackening brittlegill

Russula nigricans · 稀褶红菇
Occasional红菇科🔥 Edible With Preparation

Sources report it requires expert processing before it can be eaten; cases of poisoning from improper handling have been recorded.

The same species can be described differently across regions and stages of maturity, and countless look-alike species cannot be told apart by eye. Never use the information on this page to judge whether a real mushroom is safe to eat.

How to Tell

  • Cap grayish white to brown, bruising red then slowly blackening
  • Gills thick and widely spaced, the most sparse of any Russula
  • Old fruitbodies blacken entirely and can persist into the following year

Fun Fact

A bruise turns red first, then black, until the whole mushroom looks charred; the deadly rank russula only turns red and never black — a critical difference.
