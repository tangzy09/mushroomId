Ergot

Claviceps purpurea · 麦角菌
Rare麦角菌科⚠️ Poisonous

Reference sources list this as a poisonous mushroom.

The same species can be described differently across regions and stages of maturity, and countless look-alike species cannot be told apart by eye. Never use the information on this page to judge whether a real mushroom is safe to eat.

How to Tell

  • Purplish black, horn-shaped and hard, longer and more curved than a grain
  • Surface rough and lined; interior white when broken open
  • Replaces grain kernels on rye, wheat, and wild grass heads in autumn

Fun Fact

It replaces a grain kernel with a purplish-black sclerotium of its own. In medieval Europe, eating bread baked from ergot-contaminated rye triggered mass poisonings known as 'St. Anthony's Fire.'
