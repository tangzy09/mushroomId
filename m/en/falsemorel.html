False morel

Gyromitra esculenta · 鹿花菌
Occasional平盘菌科☠️ Deadly Poisonous

Reference sources describe this as highly toxic, with recorded fatalities.

The same species can be described differently across regions and stages of maturity, and countless look-alike species cannot be told apart by eye. Never use the information on this page to judge whether a real mushroom is safe to eat.

How to Tell

  • Cap reddish brown, irregularly brain-like and wrinkled, not honeycombed
  • Stem short, stout, and white, chambered inside
  • Grows in spring on sandy soil in conifer forests

Fun Fact

It contains gyromitrin, which the body converts into monomethylhydrazine — the same compound used as rocket fuel. Its cap is a solid, brain-like mass of folds, while a true morel's is hollow and honeycombed.
